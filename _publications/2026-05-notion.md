abstract: |
  The rapid growth of artificial intelligence, particularly large language models
  (LLMs), has led to soaring compute and energy demands, making single-datacenter
  training increasingly impractical. As a result, workloads are being distributed
  across geographically separated datacenters (DCs) interconnected by wide-area
  networks (WANs). However, WAN latency, limited bandwidth, and time-varying capacity
  constrain collective communication libraries (CCLs), which implement distributed
  communication primitives such as all-reduce and all-gather to synchronize and
  aggregate model updates across workers, and are typically optimized for intra-DC
  networks. We present NOTION, a WAN-aware synthesizer that combines fast heuristic
  planning with short-horizon lookahead to achieve low-latency scheduling, avoiding
  global topology reconstruction and ILP-scale replanning, yet maintaining performance
  close to optimal. NOTION also addresses the local greedy decision issue that can
  lead to head-of-line (HOL) blocking and inefficient link utilization in WAN
  environments. In cross-DC simulations, NOTION achieves planning speeds up to 104×
  faster than TE-CCL, reduces completion time by 32% compared to TACOS and by up to
  72% compared to NCCL, while maintaining average completion times within 22% of
  TE-CCL.
